Instagram Reels engagement rate (ER) measures how much an audience interacted with a Reel relative to the creator's follower count, usually as (likes + comments) divided by followers, expressed as a percentage. ER matters more than raw views when you analyze competitor Reels because a 1M-view post on a 10M-follower account can underperform a 200K-view post on a 50K-follower account. In our June 2026 study of 420 top Reels across 14 creators, engagement rate separated real packaging winners from fame-driven view counts.

## How to calculate Instagram Reels engagement rate

**Basic formula:**

`Engagement rate (%) = ((likes + comments) / followers) × 100`

Some teams add saves or shares when available. For public competitor research, likes + comments vs followers is the most consistent cross-account signal because saves are not always visible.

| Metric | What it tells you |
| --- | --- |
| Views | Reach and distribution |
| Likes + comments | Active response |
| ER | Response relative to audience size |
| Duration | Whether the hook held attention long enough |

SpyReels exports include ER per row so you can sort a competitor's library without manual math.

## Why views mislead competitor research

Views reward distribution and existing audience. A creator with millions of followers can post a mediocre Reel and still clear six or seven figures in views.

## When to prioritize ER vs views

**Prioritize ER when:**

- Reverse-engineering competitor hooks and formats
- Comparing creators of different sizes
- Building a swipe file of **packaging** winners
- Choosing which Reel to remake first

**Views still matter when:**

- Studying distribution at scale on similarly sized accounts
- Tracking a trend sound or topic wave
- Reporting reach to stakeholders who think in impressions

For weekly research, sort by ER first, then use views as a tiebreaker.

## How to improve your own Reels ER

1. **Study high-ER competitors** in your niche, not only famous names.
2. **Fix line one** before editing. ER starts at the hook.
3. **Match format to hook** (talking head for opinions, b-roll for stories).
4. **Test thumbnail text** as a second headline.
5. **Refresh research monthly** so you copy current winners.

## ER limitations (be honest)

ER ignores watch time and skip rate on other people's accounts (private Insights only). ER can favor smaller accounts. One great ER Reel does not prove causation. Use ER to **prioritize what to study**, then validate with your own posting tests.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a good engagement rate on Instagram Reels?

It varies by niche and size. In competitor research, compare ER **within** similar follower bands. Our study used top-30-per-creator slices, so averages are high by definition.

### Is engagement rate more important than views?

For learning what packaging works on competitor accounts, yes. For bragging rights or ad reach, views still matter.

### How do I get engagement rate for someone else's Reels?

Multiply visible likes and comments, divide by their public follower count, or use a spy/export tool that calculates ER per row.

### Why do big creators have low ER on some Reels?

Large follower bases dilute ER. A "good enough" Reel can get huge views without exceptional engagement intensity.

### Can ER predict if my remake will work?

It improves your odds of studying the right source material. Your niche, face, and offer still determine results.
